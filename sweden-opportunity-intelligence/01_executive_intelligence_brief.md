# Deliverable 1: Executive intelligence brief

**Sweden Opportunity Intelligence, 2026–2030.**

| | |
|---|---|
| Reference date | 29 October 2026 |
| Research cutoff | 30 October 2026 |
| Currency | SEK (EUR/SEK 11.04, USD/SEK 9.91, GBP/SEK 13.20 at close on 29 Oct 2026) |

**Method in one paragraph.**
- **Research:** nine independent workstreams (macro, regulation, 28 sectors, demand signals, foreign models, and three competitor studies), drawing on 634 cited sources.
- **Opportunities:** a universe of 52 opportunities, all derived from that evidence.
- **Analysis:** bottom-up sizing and 36-month models for the top 20, and a weighted ranking on the prescribed criteria.
- **Challenge:** an **independent adversarial review** that re-ranked the top 10.
- **Labels:** facts, estimates and model outputs are labelled throughout.
- **What was not done:** no customer interviews (contacting organisations was out of scope), so willingness to pay is inferred from observed prices and spending.

## 1. What is changing in Sweden?

1. **The cycle has turned, but the 2026 boom is partly borrowed.**
   - GDP grew 3.3% y/y in Q2 2026, and the consensus forecast is 2.4–3.0% for 2026–27.
   - Stimulus props up 2026: a temporary food-VAT cut, electricity-tax cuts and youth payroll-tax cuts, all expiring by end-2027.
   - The Riksbank and Konjunkturinstitutet expect rates to rise from 1.75% to about 2.4–2.5% by end-2027.
   - Real disposable-income growth slows to 0.7% in 2027.
   - **Implication:** treat consumer exuberance as cyclical (W01).
2. **The state is the fastest-growing customer.**
   - Defence spending (expenditure area 6) rises from SEK 128bn in 2024 to **SEK 225bn in 2026**, targeting 3.5% of GDP by 2030.
   - A civil-defence package of about **SEK 12bn** runs over 2026–28, rising to about SEK 15bn/year from 2028.
   - Other multi-year frames: water and sewer (SEK 590bn needed to 2040), transport maintenance (SEK 1,171bn plan 2026–37) and the grid (Svenska kraftnät SEK 95bn over 10 years) (W01, W02).
3. **Regulation has moved from proposal to enforcement.**
   - In force: Cybersäkerhetslagen (NIS2, 15 Jan 2026); CRA reporting (Sep 2026); EPBD changes (Jul and Oct 2026); PPWR (Aug 2026).
   - Coming: the Machinery Regulation (Jan 2027), the CER law (proposed Jan 2027), AMLR (Jul 2027), and full CRA application (Dec 2027).
   - Meanwhile EU simplification has **gutted** CSRD demand for SMEs and delayed AI Act high-risk duties to 2027–28 (W02).
4. **Labour, not capital, is the binding constraint.**
   - Unemployment is 8.7%, yet 51% of recruiting employers struggle to hire.
   - Small firms cite labour costs (33%) and staff shortages (30%) as obstacles; financing barely registers (1–2%).
   - Shortages are concentrated in IT security, procurement officers, electricians, plumbers and mechanics. Payroll and admin roles are in **surplus** (W01, W05).
5. **Political uncertainty is elevated.**
   - No government had formed after the 13 Sep 2026 election, and the 2027 budget had not been tabled by the cutoff.
   - Pending Swedish bills (CER, personalliggare, pay transparency) may slip. EU regulations with direct effect will not (W01, W02).

## 3. Why now?

- **Enforcement windows open in 2026–2027:**
  - NIS2 supervision (from Nov 2026);
  - CRA reporting (Sep 2026) ahead of full application (Dec 2027);
  - the Machinery Regulation (Jan 2027);
  - kerbside packaging collection (Jan 2027);
  - AMLR (Jul 2027).
- **Supply is short:**
  - IT-security specialists, security advisers and procurement officers are shortage occupations (Arbetsförmedlingen, Jun 2026).
  - The state IT-security consultant framework lapsed in Mar 2026.
- **Public money is appropriated over several years, 2026–2028.**
- **Incumbents are mispositioned.** Premium consultancies sell by the hour; software tools come without people.

## 6. Which opportunities could become substantial businesses?

- **NIS2 → CRA → CER/säkerhetsskydd regulated-resilience firm (#1 + #7 + #4).**
  - Modelled ARR at month 36 is about SEK 11m for NIS2 alone, with CRA adding about SEK 7m.
  - Comparable Swedish firms show the ceiling: Truesec SEK 690m, Knowit Cyber SEK 192m, Secify SEK 33m.
  - CRA is EU-wide, which gives it **international** potential.
- **Micro accounting roll-up (#16).** Large revenue potential (modelled Y3 about SEK 18m), but capital-intensive and contested by PE.
- **Everything else in the top 10** is likely to become a good small company (SEK 3–10m revenue) rather than a substantial one.

## 7. Which opportunities should be avoided?

- **CSRD/ESG reporting for SMEs.** Scope cut by about 70–90% after the omnibus.
- **Platform staffing.** Coople exited the UK, Gigstr went bankrupt, and Jobandtalent lost about €120m.
- **Capital-heavy B2C energy installers.** Aira lost SEK 1.38bn on SEK 1.35bn sales; 196 solar-installer bankruptcies [L].
- **EV battery certificates.** Dekra has captured the channel.
- **Municipal waste-system software.** EDP dominates on 10-year contracts.
- **AML/KYC software and accounting practice-management software.** Both are bundled cheaply by incumbents.
- **Digital product passports before 2028.**
- **Restaurant and hospitality SaaS.** Most bankruptcy-prone sector.
- **Fintech and robotics for small teams.**

## Bottom line

**Recommended business: a Swedish NIS2 operations partner** (Deliverable 7). It is a fixed-price compliance service with a software backbone for municipal utilities and mid-size NIS2 entities, adding CRA product security in 2027.

- **Capital:** SEK ~0.8m (bootstrap) to ~1.6m (base).
- **Break-even:** month 19 in the base case [modelled].
- **Biggest risk:** incumbent IT partners bundle NIS2 "well enough".
- **Status:** a strong hypothesis, not a proven business. The 45-day validation in the [90-day roadmap](08_90_day_roadmap.md) (≥3 paid gap analyses from 40 qualified prospects) is the gate.

**What the adversarial review taught us.** In Swedish public markets, *how* money may be spent matters as much as how much exists. The SEK 700k direct-award limit, framework lock-in until 2030 and per-client security vetting removed or downgraded several opportunities that looked strong on budget alone (W10).
