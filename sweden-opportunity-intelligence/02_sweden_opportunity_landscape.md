**Cyclical vs structural.**
- **Cyclical, amplified by temporary stimulus:** the 2026 consumption surge, the construction-sentiment rebound and the new-firm boom. These fade in 2027 as the food-VAT cut, the electricity-tax cut and the youth payroll-tax cut expire while rates rise.
- **Structural, multi-year and backed by budgets:**
  - Defence: expenditure area 6 rises from SEK 128bn (2024) to **SEK 225bn (2026)**, the target is 3.5% of GDP by 2030, and a SEK 300bn borrowing frame is in place.
  - Civil defence: a package of about **SEK 12bn for 2026–28**.
  - Water and sewer (VA): SEK 590bn needed by 2040.
  - Transport: a plan of SEK 1,171bn for 2026–37, tilted toward maintenance.
  - Grid: Svenska kraftnät plans SEK 95bn over 10 years.
  - Ageing: the 85+ population grows 59% by 2035.
  - Regulation: NIS2, CER, CRA, EPBD and PPWR.

**What a founder should take from this:**
1. B2C ventures that depend on 2026 consumer exuberance face a 2027 cliff.
2. Capital is not the binding constraint for SMEs; labour is. Pitches that save labour have a buyer, but national surpluses in payroll and admin roles undercut "replace the scarce clerk" pitches (W05, Method 3).
3. The strongest, best-documented new money flows to the **public sector and to regulated entities**: defence, civil preparedness, cyber and buildings.

**The pattern.** The most attractive sectors for a small, technically capable founding team share three traits:
- **(a)** a legal obligation in force now, with money attached;
- **(b)** a reachable, countable buyer list, either fragmented or mid-size;
- **(c)** incumbents that are either premium consultancies priced by the hour, or software tools without people.

Three sectors fit:
- **Civil preparedness and security protection (säkerhetsskydd)**
- **Cyber compliance (NIS2/CRA)**
- **Accounting-firm infrastructure**

**What the large markets lack.** Retail, hospitality, logistics, insurance and fintech are larger, but they lack at least two of these traits.
