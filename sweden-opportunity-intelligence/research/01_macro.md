## 2. Narrative

### 2.1 Where the cycle is: the recession is ending, but recovery is uneven
- **Output.** Sweden exited a roughly three-year stagnation. SCB describes "zero growth from Q3 2022 to Q3 2024" [SCB Sveriges ekonomi, Sep 2026]. GDP grew +1.6% q/q in Q2 2026 (+3.3% y/y calendar-adjusted). That was much stronger than most EU countries, and growth was broad across consumption, investment and exports. The GDP indicator released today puts Q3 2026 at about +2.8% y/y. August was strong, July and September were weaker.
- **Forecasts.** KI forecasts 3.0% growth in 2026, 2.4% in 2027 and 1.7% in 2028. The output gap closes in 2027–28, and KI headlines "Lågkonjunkturen ebbar ut". The Riksbank is similar: 2.8%, 2.4% and 1.3%.
- **Data-vintage warning.** 2024 GDP was revised from 0.8% (KI, Sep 2025) to 2.0% (KI, Sep 2026). Any growth narrative built on older vintages understates 2024.
- **What drives 2026.** KI names four drivers:
  - Households, supported by real-wage gains and tax cuts: the BP26 package and the temporary food-VAT cut from 12% to 6% (1 Apr 2026 – 31 Dec 2027).
  - Exports.
  - Business investment, notably foreign-owned **data centres**. Investment in services excluding housing made up 50% of business investment in Q2 2026.
  - State consumption and investment from the **defence build-up**.
- **Labour market lags.** Recovery has been productivity-led: hours worked +0.2% in 2026. Hiring so far is mostly in the public sector (defence, justice, care). LFS unemployment is still 8.7% seasonally adjusted (Sep 2026). Arbetsförmedlingen does not expect pre-recession unemployment until H2 2027. Long-term unemployment stays high: about 150k in May 2026, 145k forecast for 2027.
- **Cyclical vs structural.** Most of 2026's strength in consumption, construction sentiment and bankruptcy normalisation is **cyclical recovery amplified by temporary fiscal stimulus**. Food VAT, the electricity-tax cut and lower payroll taxes for young workers carry **sunset dates in 2027**, and real disposable income growth slows to 0.7% in 2027 [KI]. The **structural** components are:
  - Defence and preparedness spending.
  - Data-centre and electrification capex.
  - An ageing population.
  - AI diffusion.
  - Infrastructure and water reinvestment.

### 2.2 Inflation, rates and the krona: the next phase is tighter
- CPIF was 1.5% in Sep 2026, up from 0.7% in Aug. The level is artificially low because of temporary tax cuts. The Riksbank says underlying inflation is "relatively close to 2%" once fiscal effects are removed.
- Energy is the swing factor. Electricity was +17% y/y in Sep 2026, and the Riksbank cites the Middle East war, Hormuz shipping disruption and a weaker krona since Feb 2026.
- KI expects CPIF of 2.6% in 2027, "clearly above target". The Riksbank expects 2.1%.
- **Implication.** The policy rate has been 1.75% since Sep 2025 and is set to **rise**: about 2.4–2.5% by end-2027 (Riksbank and KI). KI says it does not expect rate cuts for years. Ventures relying on cheap capital or on household refinancing relief face a less benign 2027 than 2026.

### 2.3 Firms: births booming, deaths plateaued at a high level
- **Bankruptcies** rose every year from 2021 (6,463) to a 30-year-high plateau of about 10,700 in 2024–2025.
  - In 2023, the hardest-hit sector relative to its size was **hotel and restaurant**: 2.3% of firms went bankrupt. Construction and retail followed at 1.4%.
  - In 2026 YTD, bankruptcies are −2% and employees affected −37%. Restaurants show the biggest absolute decline; **Information and communication shows the biggest absolute increase**.
  - September 2026 ticked up to +8% y/y, led by finance, real estate and business services (192 firms).
  - Regionally, Stockholm County has the highest bankruptcy rate (1.2% of firms in 2025).
- **New-firm formation** is accelerating. It rose 27% y/y in Q2 2026, with increases in all 21 sectors.
  - **Programming and IT services rose +86%** (Q2) and +76% (H1). This is plausibly AI-tool-enabled micro-firm formation, which is an inference, not a measured link.
  - Formation is concentrated in metro regions, with Stockholm +32%.
  - Health and care new-firm formation fell for the fourth straight year: 2,925 in 2022 → 2,183 in 2025.
- **SMEs.** Småföretagsbarometern (Oct 2026) shows the strongest optimism since 2022.
  - The binding constraints have shifted from demand to **labour costs (33%)**, **finding staff (30%)** and **political unpredictability (29%)**.
  - "Financing difficulties" is named the single biggest obstacle by only about 1–2%. This is a proxy suggesting SME credit availability is **not** currently binding. I could not access Almi or bank lending-survey data to confirm this (see §6).
  - "High electricity prices or grid capacity" is the top obstacle for about 0%. For SMEs, energy is not the binding issue; for large industry it may be.

### 2.4 Government spending: what is budgeted (BP26) and what is uncertain (2027)
BP26 (prop. 2025/26:1, 18 Sep 2025) reforms. The main lines below are in SEK bn for 2026 / 2027 / 2028 [P].
- **Household support**
  - Income-tax cuts: 21.4 / 21.4 / 21.4.
  - Food VAT cut: 15.94 / 21.25 / 0.
  - Electricity-tax cut: 6.52 / 6.60 / 6.68.
  - Electricity and gas high-cost protection: 1.0 (2026 only).
- **Employers**: lower payroll tax for young workers, 6.05 / 5.59 / 0.
- **Welfare**
  - Healthcare queues: 2.26 / 2.23 / 1.92.
  - Eldercare competence: 0.21 / **1.99** / 0.17.
  - Psychiatry and suicide prevention: 1.56 / 1.89 / 1.59.
- **Justice**: Kriminalvården and penalties, 0.75 / 2.20 / **5.83**.
- **Civil defence and cyber**
  - Civil defence: 1.22 / 2.21 / 2.53 (BP26 table line).
  - Cyber: 0.37 / 0.41 / 0.46.
  - NIS2 implementation in municipalities and regions: 0.25 bn/yr. A separate ~50 MSEK/yr for implementing the new cybersecurity law and NIS2.
- **Energy and climate**
  - New nuclear: 1.18 / 1.26 / 3.30.
  - Klimatklivet: 1.50 / 1.54 / 1.54.
  - Kraftlyftet (grid and flexibility): 0.25 / 0.75 / 1.00.
  - Energy efficiency of small houses: 0.30 per year.
- **AI and digitalisation**: 0.54 / 0.57 / 0.59.
- **Totals**
  - Reforms excluding defence and Ukraine: 78.8 / 90.2 / 66.4.
  - Defence top-ups: 12.15 / 16.21 / 18.07.
  - Ukraine: 36.09 / 44.53 / 5.02.
- **Defence**
  - Expenditure area 6 (defence and crisis preparedness): 128.2 bn SEK in 2024 [O], 168.3 bn in 2025 [F], **225.0 bn in 2026** [P], 234.0 bn in 2027 and 207.2 bn in 2028 [P, "beräknat"]. The 2028 figure dips before later top-ups.
  - Target: **3.5% of GDP for military defence by 2030** under NATO's definition, plus 1.5% for defence-related investment, for 5% in total.
  - Financing: a cross-party agreement allows up to **300 bn SEK** of temporary borrowing for defence, of which up to **50 bn SEK** for infrastructure and supply preparedness.
- **Civil-defence package** (press release 16 Sep 2025): about **12 bn SEK over 2026–2028** (3.0 / 4.2 / 4.6).
  - Health and care: ~4.9 bn.
  - Municipalities and regions: ~4.6 bn, including meal-service preparedness of 0.75 bn/yr from 2027.
  - Food and drinking-water emergency stocks: ~2.1 bn.
  - Shelters and rescue services: ~1.2 bn.
  - Decentralised supply preparedness (food, medicine, fuel): ~1.0 bn/yr.
  - Separately, about 0.7 / 1.8 / 2.1 bn of previously parked civil-defence funds are released.
  - Total-defence emergency credit raised from 40 to 70 bn SEK.
- **ROT deduction.** The subsidy was temporarily raised from 30% to **50% for 12 May – 31 Dec 2025** (prop. 2024/25:156). It reverted to 30% from 2026 [O]. Contemporaneous critiques from ESV and Tillväxtanalys called it costly with unclear effects (Realtid, 15 Apr 2025).
- **Infrastructure.** National transport plan frame for **2026–2037: 1,171 bn SEK** (2025 prices), +21% in real terms versus 2022–2033. 607 bn goes to development, 354 bn to road maintenance (+48% real) and 210 bn to rail maintenance [P, prop. 2024/25:28].
- **2027.** The budget room for 2027–2030 is about 120 bn SEK according to KI. Statskontoret/ESV (as reported by Placera, 8 Sep 2026) sees "no room for unfinanced policy in 2027". With no government in place, the **2027 budget is the single biggest near-term policy uncertainty**. The Moderates' finance minister warned of a "reform-less, lost year" (EFN, 9 Sep 2026).

### 2.5 Regional picture
- **Stockholm** (Länsstyrelsen, Sep 2026):
  - Employment +34,100 y/y; employment rate 74.2%.
  - But unemployment is 8.9%, above the national rate.
  - New firms +32% (Q2 2026) and the highest per-capita formation (4.1 per 1,000 in Q2), yet also the highest bankruptcy rate (1.2%).
  - AI adoption is highest in Stockholm and Västsverige, and the gap to other regions is widening [SCB AISE].
- **North (Norrbotten/Västerbotten).**
  - Small firms in Mellansverige and Norrland report the strongest conditions [Småföretagsbarometern].
  - But Västerbotten had the **largest population decline of any county in H1 2026 (−0.5%)** [SCB]. This is consistent with the aftermath of **Northvolt**: Chapter 11 in the US on 21 Nov 2024, Swedish bankruptcy on 12 Mar 2025, Skellefteå production wound down from June 2025, assets bought by Lyten (announced 7 Aug 2025) [Wikipedia, M reliability].
  - I could not verify the current status of Stegra (Boden green steel) or LKAB investment figures this session (see §6).
  - **Treat "northern green-industrial boom" claims as contested.**
- **Göteborg region** (Business Region Göteborg/KI, Q2 2026): construction indicator 90.1 (weak), retail 108.2 (strong). Bankruptcies Feb–Apr 2026 were +2%, but affected employees **+54%**, with transport +140 employees.
- **South.** Sydsverige and Småland have a slower small-firm recovery [Småföretagsbarometern].

## 4. Cyclical vs structural: summary judgement
- **Mainly cyclical, plus temporary stimulus.** Covers the 2026 consumption surge, construction sentiment rebound, bankruptcy plateau and decline, SME optimism and the new-firm boom (partly). Expect fading in 2027 as stimulus expires, CPIF rises to 2.1–2.6% and the policy rate rises to about 2.4–2.5%.
- **Structural, multi-year, with public money behind it.**
  - Defence and civil preparedness: 225 bn SEK UO6 in 2026, a 300 bn borrowing frame, 12 bn civil package.
  - VA reinvestment: 590 bn to 2040.
  - Transport maintenance: 1,171 bn over 2026–37.
  - Grid: SvK 95 bn over 10 years.
  - Ageing: 85+ population +59% by 2035.
  - AI diffusion: 35% of firms, with expertise the binding barrier.
- **Contested or fragile.**
  - Northern green-industrial build-out (Northvolt collapse; Västerbotten population falling).
  - Housing new-build recovery ("very cautious").
  - The scale of 2027 fiscal choices (no government yet).
