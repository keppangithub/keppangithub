# 06 — Foreign business models with documented traction: transferability to Sweden

Reference date: 29 October 2026. Retrieval date for all sources: 2026-10-29.
Method: web search (summarised search results), plus direct fetches for key claims. Every figure has a Source Ledger ID [Sx]. Where no primary figure could be found, the text says so. Third-party estimates (Sacra, Latka, Similarweb and similar) are labelled as estimates. **Nothing has been invented. Gaps are marked "not found".**

Scoring: **Transferability (1–10)** = how attractive it would be for a new entrant to bring the model to Sweden now. It combines (a) a real Swedish pain point, (b) how much white space is left given Swedish incumbents, (c) regulatory and purchasing fit, and (d) evidence that the unit economics work abroad. A low score often means "already here" rather than "bad model".

## 2. Per-model detail

### 1. Compliance-as-a-service (NIS2 / ISO 27001), hybrid software plus a human vCISO — Score 8
- **Model:** SaaS that connects to cloud, HR and IT systems, collects evidence for ISO 27001, SOC 2 and NIS2, and is sold with auditors and partners. DataGuard (DE) bundles software with outsourced DPO/ISO experts ("GDPR-as-a-service").
- **Traction:** Vanta crossed $300M ARR with >16,000 customers, ARR +63% YoY (Fortune, 29 Apr 2026) [S6]. Drata said it passed $100M ARR (early 2025; company and third-party mix) [S6]. DataGuard has 4,000+ customers in 50+ countries and raised a €61M Series B. Its revenue is unknown (Owler's $5–25M range is an estimate) [S8].
- **Unit economics:** Not public. Vanta's growth suggests a strong mid-market ACV, but no Swedish pricing benchmark was found. WF ISMS lists SEK 3,990/month [S9]. Isodora advertises from SEK 500/month for small businesses [S9].
- **Swedish situation:** The Cybersäkerhetslag (2025:1506) entered into force on 15 January 2026 [S9]. That widens the number of in-scope entities, including municipal companies, energy, water, manufacturing and digital providers. Local tools are small: Fidureon, WF ISMS (Webbfabriken), Isodora, Noru, Stratsys [S9]. Vanta and Drata sell into Sweden in English. Kertos (DE) markets NIS2 automation [S9].
- **Why not big here yet:** Mid-market Swedish buyers prefer consultancies (Big-4, Truesec and others; not quantified). Swedish-language templates and mapping to MSB/NIS2 guidance are missing from the US tools. Public-sector buyers must use LOU procurement.
- **Rationale:** A regulatory forcing function is in force now, the incumbents are subscale, and a services-heavy model suits Swedish purchasing culture. The risk is that global tools localise. The best angle is a "done-for-you" service for 50–500-employee NIS2 entities, with fixed price per entity and Swedish templates.

### 2. Defence-SME supplier onboarding / assurance service — Score 8
- **Model:** In the UK, JOSCAR (run by Hellios) gives SMEs one onboarding questionnaire for all primes. Primes such as BAE, QinetiQ and GD UK publish SME prerequisites: Cyber Essentials, DEFCON/ITAR, ISO and social value [S10]. Hellios runs a free SME portal. Value is captured through prime membership fees and assurance services.
- **Traction:** Two dozen primes and about 6,000 SMEs on JOSCAR. That figure may be dated, and no revenue was found [S10].
- **Swedish situation:** FMV signed contracts with 170 companies last year. 50% were Swedish and 80% of those were SMEs (FMV GD quote, Affärsvärlden) [S11]. FMV's authorisation frame rises from SEK 92bn to SEK 244bn. SMEs struggle with bank loans [S12]. SOFF's 2026 evaluation says the path in for new actors "fortfarande svår", citing complex rules, long decision paths and high costs [S12]. **No Swedish JOSCAR-equivalent or commercial onboarding service was found.**
- **Why not here yet:** The defence budget ramp only started in 2024–26, and before that demand was too small. Security clearance (säkerhetsskyddsavtal) and export-control know-how is scarce.
- **Rationale:** Rapid budget growth, a documented SME barrier and no visible intermediary. A hybrid model would fit: consultancy plus a compliance-evidence platform covering säkerhetsskydd, ISO 9001/27001, ITAR/EAR and NATO AQAP, with FMV/Saab/BAE Bofors supplier packs. Unit economics are unproven. Validate with SOFF and primes.

### 3. Channel-only managed security for MSPs — Score 6
- **Model:** EDR/ITDR/SIEM with a 24/7 human SOC, sold only via MSPs with no seat minimum. Pricing is about $8.99 per endpoint per month (Sacra) [S13].
- **Traction:** $250M ARR and 270,000 businesses protected, +65% YoY (company, 30 Jul 2026) [S13].
- **Swedish situation:** Many small IT-partner MSPs. Huntress itself can sell here through them. NIS2 supply-chain requirements push MSPs to offer SOC services.
- **Rationale:** The model is proven, but the global vendor already covers Sweden. The transferable opportunity is a Swedish MSP-enablement layer: Swedish-language SOC, NIS2 reporting and insurer-aligned controls. Score 6.

### 4. AI-native accounting firm / buying client books — Score 6
- **Model (US):** Pilot runs bookkeeping as software plus offshore and onshore accountants. Basis sells AI agents to accounting firms and is used by about 30% of the top-25 US firms [S3].
- **Traction:** Basis raised a $100M Series B at $1.15B (Feb 2026), about $138M raised in total [S3]. Pilot's revenue is only estimated: $27M in 2024 (Latka) vs ~$43M ARR in 2022 (Sacra). The figures conflict [S2].
- **Swedish situation:** **Wint** already executes the hybrid. It is an automated bookkeeping service that buys client books from accounting firms, with a planned SEK 50M of purchases [S14]. In 2026 it acquired the Gothenburg firms 8082 and HRed (28 staff) [S14]. It reportedly swung to "tens of millions SEK" profit under Norvestor ownership (Techleap, Jun 2025; page returned 403, summary only) [S15]. Revenue rose from SEK 83.6M (2021) to about SEK 151.6M (latest year in Bolagsfakta's table; year labels unclear) [S14].
- **Why gap remains:** Sweden has about 4,000+ small byråer with ageing owners (count **not verified** this pass). Fortnox/Bokio cover DIY users.
- **Rationale:** Proven locally by Wint, but room exists for vertical specialists (construction, restaurants, healthcare) or a Basis-style tool for byråer in Swedish (K2/K3, SIE). Score 6.

### 5. PE roll-up of accounting practices — Score 5
- **Traction (UK):** Xeinadin made 22 acquisitions in the year to May 2025. The £800m+ auction later collapsed (Accountancy Age, Feb 2026) [S4][S5]. Cooper Parry moved from Waterland to Lee Equity [S5].
- **Sweden:** Already consolidated at the top. Aspia was formed from PwC and KPMG units (IK, later Vitruvian). Azets (Hg/PAI) bought KPMG Sweden's SME audit and advisory unit with about 270 staff (2025) [S16]. Ludvig & Co is also active [S48].
- **Rationale:** The model works but is crowded, and UK exits show multiple compression. A smaller "AI-first sub-scale roll-up" variant is still viable. Score 5.

### 6. Insurer/BRF-distributed leak detection — Score 6
- **Model:** HomeServe LeakBot is a clip-on pipe sensor running on SIGFOX with a 5-year battery. Insurers distribute it to cut escape-of-water claims [S17].
- **Traction:** Aviva, RSA (More Th>n) and L&G deployed it; Ecclesiastical trialled it [S17]. Water leaks were reported as over 20% of Aviva's home claims. **No household count or revenue found.**
- **Sweden:** Water damage is a major BRF and villa cost. Länsförsäkringar offers regional, undated subsidies (Västerbotten: SEK 2,000 plus 10% premium discount) [S18]. A 2016 Uppsala pilot ran with Vatette valves [S18]. No nationwide insurer-distributed sensor programme was found.
- **Why not here:** Fragmented regional insurers (the LF federation of 23 companies), and the BRF purchasing cycle runs through the board.
- **Rationale:** Distribution via BRF managers (Nabo, Boappa) plus insurers is plausible. The hardware is commoditised, so the value lies in claims data and partnerships. Score 6.

### 7. Acquiring service books from bankrupt installers — Score 6
- **Model:** Buy the customer lists and assets of insolvent solar or heat-pump installers cheaply and convert orphaned owners to service subscriptions (Otovo Care).
- **Traction:** Otovo bought Soly NL's customers and assets for about €0.75M, covering 24,000 installations (Dec 2025). It pays NOK 1,000 per Solcellespesialisten customer who signs up for Otovo Care, out of about 5,000 customers [S19].
- **Sweden:** Many installed PV systems date from the 2021–23 boom. **Data on Swedish installer bankruptcies was not collected this pass, so this needs verification.** Aira's distress (row 18) shows the risk of orphaned heat pumps.
- **Rationale:** Low capex and countercyclical. Score 6, conditional on verifying the bankruptcy pipeline.

### 8. Public-procurement intelligence and bid writing — Score 5
- **Traction:** Stotles raised a $13M Series A (May 2025) to enter the US and claims to double customers' win rate (company claim) [S20]. Tussell raised a £1m seed (2017/18); no later rounds found [S21].
- **Sweden:** **Tendium** has 1,400+ customers (company), est. $8M ARR (Sep 2025, third-party), was on Deloitte Fast 50 2025, and charges from €125/month [S22]. Mercell and Visma Opic are incumbent tender databases (not re-verified).
- **Rationale:** The software layer is taken (3). A done-for-you, AI-assisted bid-writing service for SMEs facing LOU tenders is less contested (6). Average 5.

### 9. Tech-enabled home-care operator — Score 5
- **Traction:** Cera reached $500M annualised revenue (May 2025, company), with $100M+ from non-home-care lines, and raised $150M debt plus equity (Jan 2025) [S23]. An older claim says it was profitable at $120M revenue. Honor bought Home Instead, a network of about $2.1B 2020 revenue, 1,200 locations and 90,000 caregivers [S24].
- **Sweden:** Municipal LOV choice systems allow private hemtjänst in some municipalities. Personal alarm provider Careium had 854.1 MSEK net sales in 2025 (−1.9%) and missed guidance [S25].
- **Why not here:** Prices are set by municipalities, LOV is not used in all municipalities, and margins are thin. (Structural claims are from general knowledge and not re-verified this pass.)
- **Rationale:** Operator plus software can win on staff retention, but municipal price-setting caps the upside. Score 5.

### 10. Property / BRF management software — Score 5

- **Sweden:** Nabo is a digital-first BRF manager with about 2,600–4,000 BRFs (sources vary) and in Jul 2026 acquired BoNea and Akevo [S27]. Boappa (OurLiving) has 2,000+ paying customers and 200,000 users [S28]. SBC, Riksbyggen and HSB are incumbents (not re-verified).

## 3. Cross-cutting observations
1. **Regulation-forced B2B services score highest.** NIS2/Cybersäkerhetslagen and the defence ramp-up are the strongest "why now" triggers. Both favour hybrid service-plus-software models over pure SaaS, which matches Swedish purchasing of trusted advisors.
2. **Capital-heavy B2C energy installers are in distress across the Nordics and Benelux.** That includes Aira (going-concern doubt), Soly NL and Solcellespesialisten NO. The opportunity is the **aftermarket** (row 7), not the installer itself.
3. **Platform staffing is retreating** (Coople UK exit, Gigstr bankruptcy, Jobandtalent net loss). Avoid it.
4. **Sweden already has local winners** in many categories: Wint, Lexly, Tendium, Nabo, Boappa, Next, Fenix, Flower, Inrego and Klaravik. For these, the model is "arrived", not "missing".
5. **Data gaps to close:** Swedish solar/heat-pump installer bankruptcies 2024–26, the BRF count and water-damage claim cost, Swedish regional rules on hired nurses, Byggnet and Entrewise traction, Mercell/Visma Opic customer counts, and 1KOMMA5° 2025 results.
