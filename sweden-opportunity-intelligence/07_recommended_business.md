# Deliverable 7: Recommended business

**Reference date:** 29 October 2026.

## Recommendation

**Launch a Swedish NIS2 operations partner:** a fixed-price compliance service with a software backbone for mid-sized organisations covered by Cybersäkerhetslagen. Start with municipal energy, water and waste companies, and private NIS2 entities with 50–500 staff.

- Use it as the platform to add **CRA product-security (PSIRT) services in 2027**.
- Add **CER resilience and säkerhetsskydd services later**, the latter only once a cleared senior adviser is on the team.

**Conviction level: moderate.** This is the highest-priority opportunity, and the evidence for demand and budget is the strongest in the study:
- the law is in force, with sanctions;
- about 1,800 entities are covered;
- there are documented hourly spend levels;
- the state IT-security framework has expired.

However, **no customer has yet been asked to pay.** The validation gates in the [90-day roadmap](08_90_day_roadmap.md) must be passed before committing more than about SEK 0.3m.

## Why it beats the alternatives

**#4 Säkerhetsskydd retainer** (61.0) has larger headline public money, but:
- the national framework is closed until 2030;
- direct awards are capped at SEK 700k;
- each client requires security vetting, and founders need clearances;
- it is unprofitable in Y3 under every sensitivity.

It is better as a later product line of the recommended company.

**#7 CRA PSIRT** (61.0) has the most durable driver, but demand starts in earnest only in 2027. It shares skills and buyers with NIS2, so it becomes **product line 2**.

**#38 Grid-connection consultancy** (60.0) works only with power-systems engineers on the team. It is a modest project consultancy with low evidence confidence.

**Accounting-sector plays** (#13, #15, #16, #17) were weakened by the review:
- The incumbent bundle costs SEK 499/month.
- Small-firm transfers are tiny.
- The roll-up needs about SEK 6–8m and competes with PE buyers.

**BRF plays** (#27, #30) face low willingness to pay per association, or project-based economics that do not break even in the base case.

**Non-software alternatives:** the strongest (#4, #38, #30) are either founder-gated or have weak unit economics.

## What the customer pays for

The customer pays for **an outcome they are personally accountable for**: *"Our organisation is registered, its ISMS is documented, management is trained, and we can report an incident in 24 hours. When the supervisor calls, a named expert stands next to us."*

| Product | Price (hypothesis) | Basis |
|---|---|---|
| NIS2 gap analysis (fixed scope, 3–4 weeks) | SEK 60k (pilot price SEK 40k) | Opsio public SEK 45–90k (W07) |
| 90-day implementation pack | SEK 90k | Assumption below traditional SEK 450k–1.7m projects (W07, L) |
| Monthly retainer (named vCISO ~10–12 h, audit, exercise, board report) | SEK 18k/month | Opsio public SEK 10–30k/month (W07) |
| Add-ons: supplier assessments, incident readiness, CER (2027), CRA PSIRT (2027) | SEK 15–30k each or monthly | Assumption |

A two-year contract comes to about SEK 0.52m, which keeps public buyers below the SEK 700k direct-award limit (W10 §0.1).

## How to win the first customers

1. **Enumerate the market exactly.**
   - Municipal companies in SNI 35, 36–37 and 38 come from SCB/Bolagsverket. Filter for in-scope size and water-supply thresholds.
   - Private entities come from MCF sector guidance and the SCB business register (≥50 employees in Annex I/II sectors).
   - Target about 300 records.
2. **Qualify for "no CISO, has budget".** Signals include a security job ad, NIS2 in board minutes and an MSP-only IT setup.
3. **Lead with a free 45-minute board briefing** on personal liability and the 24-hour reporting duty, followed by a fixed-price gap-analysis offer.
4. **Get three discounted reference clients** (gap analysis at SEK 40k) in exchange for a case study.
5. **Partner with 2–3 regional MSPs** that lack compliance capability, so the firm complements them rather than competing.
6. **Publish a Swedish MCFFS 2026:1 control-mapping guide** to earn inbound demand.

## What could cause it to fail

1. **Bundling by incumbents.** Atea, Advania and Tietoevry fold NIS2 into existing IT contracts "well enough".
2. **Budget reality.** Public entities fund NIS2 "within existing budgets" (MCF), and private mid-size entities defer until supervision bites.
3. **Commoditisation.** AI-generated ISMS documentation (Isodora from SEK 500/month) erodes the implementation fee faster than the retainer value grows.
4. **Talent.** Senior information-security people are scarce, and founder delivery caps growth at about 15–20 retainers.
5. **Liability.** A client incident blamed on the advice. Mitigate with scope letters and professional indemnity insurance.
6. **Political or regulatory change.** Simplification at EU level (a NIS2 "omnibus") reduces obligations. No such proposal had been adopted at the reference date.

## If the evidence is insufficient

If V1 or V2 fails, **do not force the business.** The fallbacks, in priority order:
1. **CRA PSIRT for manufacturers.** Validate in Q1–Q2 2027 with the same assets.
2. **Grid-connection consultancy.** Only if a power-systems engineer co-founder joins.
